FIELD NOTE / IMPACT

From cutting to creating livelihoods

A story from the FREE Fund project on female genital mutilation/cutting (FGM/C), livelihoods and community-level change.

THE HUMAN STORY

Development work becomes more visible when we understand the people, systems and decisions behind it.

01 / The context

Research connected the persistence of FGM/C to cultural norms and economic incentives, informing an alternative-livelihood response.

The project supported vocational training for former cutters, including soap-making and pastries training, alongside certificates and seed materials.

02 / Project evidence

  • Research explored cultural norms and economic incentives associated with FGM/C
  • Former cutters received vocational training in soap-making and pastries
  • Training was accompanied by certificates and seed materials

The training and livelihood support are project activities; no long-term income or FGM/C prevalence outcome is claimed here.

03 / My contribution

Making the work visible and meaningful.

My communications contribution is demonstrated through the ability to communicate a complex gender and development issue with sensitivity to context, social norms and economic realities. The available record does not specify individual responsibility for designing or delivering the livelihood intervention.

04 / What this demonstrates

Communication that connects people, evidence and change.
