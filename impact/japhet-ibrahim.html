FIELD NOTE / IMPACT

One entrepreneur, 220+ toilets

A human story from Kwali Area Council about sanitation entrepreneurship and community action.

THE HUMAN STORY

Development work becomes more visible when we understand the people, systems and decisions behind it.

01 / The context

Training and market opportunity helped one sanitation entrepreneur expand his business while contributing to local sanitation access.

Japhet Ibrahim moved into toilet sales and construction, promoted improved toilets and constructed toilets for a Primary Healthcare Centre that previously had none. The story reports that he had sold over 220 toilets.

02 / Project evidence

  • Japhet had sold over 220 toilets, according to the documented story
  • He promoted improved toilets and undertook toilet construction
  • He constructed toilets for a Primary Healthcare Centre that previously had none

The 220+ sales figure belongs to Japhet’s business and the project story. It is not a personal sales result or a figure attributable to my communications work.

03 / My contribution

Making the work visible and meaningful.

I interviewed Japhet in my Communications Officer role and developed his experience into an impact story, making a wider sanitation-marketing effort tangible through one person’s journey.

04 / What this demonstrates

Communication that connects people, evidence and change.
